# Research Scope & Goal Description: Venezuela Productivity Trajectory (1970–2026)

## 1. Primary Empirical Question
How did Venezuela's multi-sector productivity and physical output evolve across half a century (1970–2026) in both **absolute volume** and **per-capita output / labor productivity**, and what were the structural mechanisms driving divergence across key sectors?

## 2. Sectoral Scope
The investigation covers five core sectors spanning extraction, heavy manufacturing, primary forestry, and tertiary activity:
1. **Crude Oil & Condensates**: Primary extractive driver of the national economy (measured in thousand barrels/day, annual barrels per capita, and output per extractive worker).
2. **Crude Steel (SIDOR / CVG)**: Primary ferrous metallurgy (measured in thousand metric tons, kg per capita, and plant capacity utilization).
3. **Primary Aluminum (ALCASA / VENALUM / CVG)**: Non-ferrous metallurgy linked to the Caroní hydroelectric grid (measured in thousand metric tons and kg per capita).
4. **Wood & Industrial Forestry (PROFORCA / Maderas del Orinoco)**: Renewable resource sector centered on the Uverito Caribbean pine plantations (measured in thousand cubic meters $\text{m}^3$ of industrial roundwood and $\text{m}^3$ per 1,000 residents).
5. **Services & Commercial Activity**: Domestic tertiary sector, financial intermediation, and utilities (measured in constant 2015 USD / PPP-adjusted value-added, total value-added, and value-added per service worker).

## 3. Temporal Horizon & The 10 Historical Structural Breaks (1970–2026)
The 56-year trajectory is analyzed with respect to ten critical structural break events:
1. **1974 Oil Nationalization**: Creation of Petróleos de Venezuela S.A. (PDVSA) under Carlos Andrés Pérez, initiating state ownership of hydrocarbons and large-scale industrialization programs (Plan IV SIDOR).
2. **1983 Viernes Negro (Black Friday)**: End of the fixed exchange rate (4.30 Bs/USD), capital flight, onset of the Latin American debt crisis, and implementation of exchange control systems (RECADI).
3. **1989 Caracazo**: Severe social unrest following IMF structural adjustment package (*El Gran Viraje*), price deregistrations, and transport fare hikes.
4. **February 4, 1992 Coup d'État Attempt**: Military rebellion led by Hugo Chávez against Carlos Andrés Pérez, inaugurating severe political instability and institutional delegitimation.
5. **1994 Banco Latino Crisis**: Systemic banking collapse affecting $>50\%$ of the commercial banking system, triggering inflation and capital controls.
6. **December 1998 Election of Hugo Chávez**: Transition from the Fourth Republic (*Puntofijo*) to the Fifth Republic (*Revolución Bolivariana*), followed by the 1999 Constitution.
7. **April 11, 2002 Coup d'État Attempt**: Temporary ousting of Chávez, institutional crisis, and deepening ideological polarization.
8. **2002–2003 PDVSA Oil Strike (*Paro Petrolero*)**: General strike resulting in the dismissal of $>18{,}000$ experienced technical, managerial, and operational PDVSA personnel, fundamentally restructuring corporate governance and capital expenditure.
9. **March–April 2013 Succession of Nicolás Maduro**: Transition following Chávez's death, coinciding with the collapse of global oil prices, tightening FX controls (CADIVI/CENCOEX), and subsequent hyperinflation.
10. **2010s–2026 U.S. Sanctions & Financial Restrictions**: Escalating unilateral coercive measures including 2015 EO 13692, 2017 financial debt issuance restrictions (EO 13808), 2019 PDVSA trade sanctions (EO 13850), and subsequent Chevron general license waivers (GL 41, 44) through 2026.

## 4. Normalization & Denominator Standards
In accordance with `responsible-research` and `responsible-demographics`:
- **Physical Output vs. Nominal Distortions**: Commodities are tracked primarily in physical units to avoid oil-price-boom nominal illusions (e.g. 1979–1981, 2004–2014). Services are measured in constant 2015 USD and PPP.
- **Demographic Denominator Tiers**:
  - **Tier 0**: Unrevised linear projection ($>34\text{M}$ by 2025).
  - **Tier 1 (Core Empirical)**: Migration-adjusted resident population ($~28.3\text{M}–28.9\text{M}$) incorporating UN WPP 2024 revisions and ENCOVI data for the post-2015 net emigration of $>7.7\text{ million}$ individuals.
  - **Tier 2**: Active working-age population (15–64) and sectoral employment counts to compute direct labor productivity.
