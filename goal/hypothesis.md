# Falsifiable Hypotheses & Structural Mechanisms: Venezuela Productivity (1970–2026)

## Hypothesis 1: Resource Curse & Early Crowding Out (1974–1989)
* **Statement ($H_{1}$)**: The 1974 oil nationalization and subsequent petro-dollar influx created classic Dutch Disease dynamics, overvaluing the real exchange rate and depressing non-oil tradeable productivity (forestry, agriculture, downstream metallurgy) prior to the 1983 Viernes Negro currency collapse.
* **Mechanism**: State-directed capital allocation favored heavy state enterprises (CVG SIDOR, ALCASA) at the expense of flexible light manufacturing and services, generating high capital-output ratios and vulnerable debt structures.
* **Testable Prediction**: Per-capita physical output in basic industries stalls or turns negative during 1978–1983 despite elevated nominal GDP.

## Hypothesis 2: Human Capital & Technical Governance Disruption (2002–2003)
* **Statement ($H_{2}$)**: The mass dismissal of $\sim 18{,}000$ engineers, geologists, and managers from PDVSA during the 2002–2003 strike triggered a permanent structural downward shift in extractive efficiency, accelerating reservoir depletion rates and elevating decline curves.
* **Mechanism**: Loss of institutional memory, specialized reservoir-pressurization expertise, and preventative maintenance protocols impaired operational efficiency even during the 2004–2014 oil price super-cycle.
* **Testable Prediction**: Decline in crude oil output per worker and drilling rig productivity emerges years before the 2014 oil price collapse and before international financial sanctions.

## Hypothesis 3: Reinvestment Starvation & Expropriation Cascade (2007–2012)
* **Statement ($H_{3}$)**: The renationalization of SIDOR (2008), expropriation of agricultural and forestry concessions, and diversion of CVG/PDVSA cash flows to off-budget social funds (FONDEN) induced catastrophic capex starvation across metallurgy, aluminum, and forestry.
* **Mechanism**: Operational revenues were depleted for current public consumption and subsidized imports, preventing re-investment in potline maintenance (Alcasa/Venalum), blast furnace refractory linings (SIDOR), and electric transmission infrastructure (Guri SEN).
* **Testable Prediction**: Steel and aluminum production per capita collapse by $>60\%$ between 2008 and 2014, while global aluminum and steel production continued to expand.

## Hypothesis 4: Multi-System Joint Failure & Infrastructure Gridlock (2013–2019)
* **Statement ($H_{4}$)**: Post-2013 output collapse represents a non-linear joint operational failure where production facilities faced simultaneous capex deficit $\cap$ power grid (SEN) rationing $\cap$ raw material input shortages $\cap$ hyperinflationary breakdown of pricing mechanisms.
* **Mechanism**: The national electric system (SEN) suffered nationwide blackouts in 2019; potlines at Venalum and Alcasa were disconnected, solidifying molten metal inside cells and effectively destroying production capacity.
* **Testable Prediction**: The joint failure rate exceeds the product of unconditional marginal failure rates, reflecting strong systemic dependency ($M_{\text{ind}}$ bounds).

## Hypothesis 5: Sanctions Compression vs. Pre-Existing Decline Gradient (2017–2026)
* **Statement ($H_{5}$)**: U.S. financial sanctions (2017) and sectoral oil sanctions (2019) severely restricted export markets, shipping logistics, and access to diluent/refining components, sharply compressing remaining cash flows; however, $>70\%$ of the per-capita output drop in oil and $>85\%$ in metallurgy occurred *prior* to the 2017 sanctions.
* **Mechanism**: Sanctions acted as an external accelerator on an already depleted and maintenance-starved physical infrastructure.
* **Testable Prediction**: Piecewise econometric structural-break regressions locate the primary break dates between 2008 and 2014, with sanctions explaining the secondary terminal contraction and post-2022 license-dependent stagnation.

## Hypothesis 6: Demographic Emigration & The Denominator Offset (2015–2026)
* **Statement ($H_{6}$)**: Failing to adjust the population denominator for the net emigration of $\sim 7.7\text{ million}$ Venezuelans (Tier 0 vs. Tier 1) artificially depresses post-2015 per-capita output by $\sim 20\text{–}25\%$, while labor-force-adjusted productivity (Tier 2) reveals a disproportionate loss of high-skill technical labor.
* **Mechanism**: Brain drain concentrated among engineers, physicians, teachers, and technical operators reduced total factor productivity (TFP) far beyond the raw reduction in headcounts.
* **Testable Prediction**: Per-capita output remains depressed by $>75\%$ across basic industries even under migration-adjusted Tier 1 population denominators.
